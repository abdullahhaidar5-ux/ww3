## War support at home (campaign runner, 2026-11-11)

- How a country is governed decides how war news lands. Families used in the game (`WS_GOV` in src/13v-gwarsup.js, simplified from the public democracy indices — https://freedomhouse.org/report/freedom-world · https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/political-regime): democracies = the nations that hold competitive national elections (`EL_DEM`, real election calendar above); monarchies = SA, AE, JO, OM, QA, KW, BH, MA, BN, SZ; theocracies = IR, AF (+ the fictional Shia/Islamic factions); party states = CN, KP, VN, CU, LA, ER; military-led governments after the 2021–2023 coups = MM, SD, ML, BF, NE, GN, TD; everything else = presidential state. **Game: bad battle news ×1.5 in a democracy, ×0.5 in a party state; war weariness ×1.3 vs ×0.6 (party states catch up after 18 months when the truth leaks); a rally in the first 6 months after an invasion (history: support surges when the homeland is attacked and fades within months); below 25% support in wartime: protests (democracies, from 30%), strikes (-5% income), or a split officer corps (military-led). Restricting war news buys 6 months and then costs 5 points.** No real leader or party is named.
- **Prices and shortages (slice 2):** in 2026 the war-hit economy, not the front, is what Iranians feel daily — households cutting essentials, and open discussion of whether economic pain turns into unrest — https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/10/iranians-cut-essentials-and-put-dreams-hold-war-hit-economy-bites · https://gulfnews.com/world/mena/will-iranians-revolt-as-economic-pain-deepens-tehran-is-already-preparing-1.500657103 — **game: while at war, hunger −3, no fuel −1, rationing −1 war support a month (democracies ×1.5, Zealous nations ×0.5); nothing in peace (unrest covers that). ## Supply lines (campaign "Real supply", slice 1 → `src/13zl-gsupply.js`, owner functions `geArmySupply` / `geSupplyStep` / `geFuelNeed`)

Real-world basis for the numbers (looked up 2026-11-11):
- **Trucks only reach so far.** Russian army truck brigades can sustain a force about 90 miles (~145 km) beyond a rail depot with two round trips a day; at 180 miles capacity halves, and it keeps falling with distance — Alex Vershinin, "Feeding the Bear", War on the Rocks, Nov 2021: https://warontherocks.com/2021/11/feeding-the-bear-a-closer-look-at-russian-army-logistics/ . In the game: full supply to ~200 km of weighted line, half at ~600 km, a trickle (20 %→10 %) past ~1,200 km. The thresholds are stretched a little because map provinces are coarse (one hop is often 300–400 km).
- **Rail carries far, but only on your own network.** Armies depend on railheads; rail on occupied ground needs repair and re-gauging (same article; Stars and Stripes on Ukraine 2022: https://www.stripes.com/theaters/europe/2022-03-04/how-ukraine-rail-network-threw-russia-military-off-track-5224091.html). In the game: never-conquered friendly land counts 35 % of its length, occupied land 100 %.
- **Long truck lines burn their own fuel.** The 1944 Red Ball Express ran ~6,000 trucks over routes that grew past 600 km and burned roughly 300,000 US gallons of fuel a day itself (HistoryNet: https://historynet.com/red-ball-express/ ; Warfare History Network: https://warfarehistorynetwork.com/red-ball-express-the-legendary-lifeline/). In the game: an army's share of national fuel burn doubles at a 600 km line (cap +150 %).
- **Seasons.** Rasputitsa (spring thaw Mar–Apr, autumn rains Oct–Nov) on the East European plain, deep winter above ~48° N, Gulf/Sahara summer heat (Jun–Aug) and the South/South-East Asian monsoon (Jun–Sep) all cut road throughput. Game values (share of supplies still arriving): mud 65 %, monsoon 70 %, winter 75 %, desert heat 85 % — estimates inside the range the sources describe, as GMOD rows (`supplyIn`), so the later "Calendar and seasons" item can retune or extend them in one place.
- Terrain line-length weights (estimates): mountains ×1.6, jungle ×1.5, snow ×1.4, desert ×1.3, farmland ×1.1; a river crossing adds 60 km. Port as a source = +250 km (sea lift, transshipment); an ally's capital = +330 km (host-nation support).
- Map scale: 13.3 km per map unit; latitude from the map's Equal Earth y (fitted on Cairo, Tehran, Kyiv, Moscow; ±1°).

## Escalation ladder (campaign runner, 2026-11-11)

- States climb in steps and usually stop: public warnings, red lines and alerts are far more common than wars, and third parties broker the way down. 2026 example: the US–Iran ceasefire of April 2026 was brokered through Pakistan's channel, with further talks later faltering — https://www.thefridaytimes.com/08-Apr-2026/war-turned-final-hours-pakistan-already-built-channel · https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/role-of-mediation-in-us-iran-ceasefire/print_manually · https://southasianvoices.org/geo-m-oth-n-pakistan-us-iran-peace-falters-08-08-2026/ · https://www.newarab.com/analysis/pakistans-diplomatic-gamble-how-unlikely-peacemaker-emerged . Base rates: only a small share of militarised disputes between states become wars (Correlates of War MID data, https://correlatesofwar.org/data-sets/mids/), and nuclear-armed states have fought each other directly only in brief limited clashes. **Game: one crisis level per pair (warning → red line → forces on alert); AI majors start a crisis about 2% of months and now climb the ladder before any war; two nuclear-armed nations stop at the red line (war chance 1% a month there); a war without a red line is a surprise attack (-10 opinion everywhere); a mediator on good terms with both sides lets both step back without losing face.**

## Escalation inside a war (campaign runner, 2026-11-11)

- Wars usually stay limited for a long time; the step to strikes on cities and oil is taken late and by the side under pressure, and the other side answers in kind. Iran–Iraq 1980–88: the tanker war grew from 1984 and the "war of the cities" came in bursts (1984, 1985, 1987, 1988) between UN-brokered pauses that both sides later broke — https://www.Britannica.com/event/Iran-Iraq-War · https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/war/iran-iraq-4.htm · https://merip.org/1984/07/chronicle-of-the-gulf-war/ · https://www.historytoday.com/history-matters/first-tanker-war — **game: an AI opens strategic strikes first in about 6% of war months (25% when it is clearly losing, 2% between two nuclear powers) and always answers them; the first to cross a rung loses opinion everywhere (blockade -3, strategic strikes -6, nuclear alert -12), the victim's public rallies; agreed limits last 6 months and breaking them costs double; an unused rung cools after 6 months.**
- Nuclear alerts are signals, are very rare, and have not been followed by use: the US went to DEFCON 3 in October 1973; Russia put its deterrent forces on a "special regime of combat duty" on 27 Feb 2022, which analysts read as a warning to outside powers rather than preparation to strike — https://www.armscontrol.org/issue-briefs/2022-02/FAQ-russia-ukraine · https://www.defenseone.com/threats/2022/02/what-just-happened-putins-nuclear-forces-heres-what-experts-say/362501 · https://www.pbs.org/newshour/amp/world/putin-put-russias-nuclear-forces-on-alert-what-does-that-mean-for-the-risk-of-nuclear-war — **game: nuclear alert is its own rung ($200 a month); a nuclear enemy matches it, a non-nuclear one gets a nuclear friend to draw a red line or, if not winning, asks for a truce; an AI goes on alert only when losing badly (10% a month) and its last-resort strike needs a month on alert first.**
